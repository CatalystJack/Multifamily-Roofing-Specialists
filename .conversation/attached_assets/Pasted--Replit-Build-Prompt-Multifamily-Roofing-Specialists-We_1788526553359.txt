Build a professional, institutional-feeling marketing website for a B2B roofing company. This is not a residential home-services site, it's a vendor site aimed at apartment developers, owners, and general contractors deciding who to award roofing work to. It should feel closer to a commercial real estate or construction-tech company than a typical local roofer's site: confident, understated, credible. ## Design system

**Color palette**
- Base background: deep navy `#0B1929`
- Secondary panel background: slightly lighter navy `#101F33` (for alternating section bands)
- Primary text: warm off-white `#F2EFE9`
- Secondary/body text: muted blue-grey `#A9B4C2`
- Accent (numbering, eyebrows, dividers, small highlights): warm amber/gold `#C9973E`
- Hairline borders/dividers: low-opacity white `rgba(242,239,233,0.12)`

Use the accent color sparingly — small numerals, thin rules, section labels, and icon strokes, not large blocks or buttons. Primary CTA buttons should be a solid off-white fill with navy text (inverted from the background), not amber, so the amber stays a quiet signature rather than a loud action color.

**Typography**
- Display/headline typeface: an elegant serif with real editorial weight, something like Freight Display, Canela, or if using Google Fonts, **"Fraunces"** or **"Playfair Display"** at a light-to-regular weight. Headlines should feel restrained and confident, not decorative.
- Body/UI typeface: a clean, neutral sans-serif such as **"Inter"** or **"Neue Montreal"** (Google Fonts substitute: Inter). Use it for nav, body copy, buttons, and labels.
- Keep body text line length under ~75 characters per line.
- Section eyebrow labels (e.g. "SIX CORE PROGRAMS") are small, letter-spaced, uppercase, amber-colored, and sit above the serif headline they introduce, matching the reference screenshot described below.

**Layout concept**
- Generous vertical whitespace between sections, alternating background bands (`#0B1929` / `#101F33`) to separate sections without hard borders everywhere
- Left-aligned text blocks, not centered — this should read like a considered editorial layout, not a centered SaaS landing page
- The "Six Core Programs" section is a 2-column x 3-row grid on desktop, single column on mobile, each item introduced with a small amber numeral (01–06), a serif sub-headline, and a short sans-serif description, separated by thin horizontal hairlines — matching the visual pattern of a reference screenshot the client provided: dark navy background, small amber "SIX CORE PROGRAMS" eyebrow, large serif hero headline top-left, and a two-column numbered grid on the right with amber numerals, serif item titles, and muted blue-grey body copy under each, separated by thin hairline rules.
- Stats bar is a simple two-up (or three-up if a third stat is added later) row with a large serif number and a small sans-serif label underneath, no icons or card backgrounds needed

## Design principles to follow

- Spend visual boldness in exactly one place: the hero serif headline. Everything else stays quiet and disciplined.
- Do not use rounded "SaaS card" treatments with soft drop shadows on every content block. Use hairline dividers and whitespace instead of boxes/cards to separate content.
- Do not default to a warm cream background or a terracotta accent color. Stick to the navy/amber palette specified above.
- Only use numbered markers (01–06) where content is genuinely a sequence, which the Six Core Programs section is.
- Motion: keep it minimal. A single subtle fade/slide on initial hero load is fine. Avoid scroll-triggered fade-ins on every section and hover-lift effects on every card, that reads as generic AI-generated motion.
- Make sure focus states are visible for keyboard navigation and color contrast is accessible (the off-white text on navy background should easily pass contrast checks).

## What "professional" means here

Before finishing, check the build against this: would a REIT asset manager or a GC's project executive take this site seriously as a vendor, or does it read like a small local contractor's template site? If anything feels like a generic home-services template (bright CTA buttons, stock photos of a roofer giving a thumbs-up, testimonial carousels with 5-star icons, countdown-style urgency banners), remove or replace it. The credibility should come from restraint, real specificity in the copy, and the editorial-serif/navy-amber visual system, not from decoration.
